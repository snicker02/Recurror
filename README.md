# Recurror

Recursive mirror drawing in the browser. The name combines *recur* and *mirror*. Every stroke is stored as an editable spline. Each stroke has its own symmetry group, recursion and warp, and is drawn with lit, shaded geometry across layers.

WebGL1, no dependencies, plain ES modules.

## Run

ES modules need to be served over HTTP. Opening the page as `file://` won't work.

```
cd recurror
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000
```

## One transform type

Every copy is an anti-Möbius map, z ↦ (a·w + b)/(c·w + d) with w = z or z̄.
- **Similarities** have c = 0. These are rotations, mirrors, glides, scalings and translations, which covers every Euclidean group, including all 17 wallpaper groups.
- **Hyperbolic disk automorphisms and circle inversions** need c ≠ 0.

The vertex shader evaluates the map for each vertex, then applies the optional warp. It multiplies the two Jacobians and uses the result for three things:
- extruding the stroke width in the right direction
- keeping anti-aliasing at one device pixel wherever the copy is scaled
- rotating the normals that drive the lighting

Copies smaller than about a pixel, or far off the artboard, are culled before drawing.

**Instancing.** With `ANGLE_instanced_arrays` (a WebGL1 extension available almost everywhere), each copy's Möbius coefficients are per-instance attributes, and a stroke draws in one call per recursion level and pass. Fills need even-odd stencilling for each copy on its own, so their copies are grouped into batches whose image circles don't overlap, with one instanced call per batch and pass. Without the extension (or with `?noinst` in the URL) it falls back to one call per copy. Both paths render pixel-identical output.

## Symmetry (per stroke)

| Mode | |
|---|---|
| Mirror, Rotational, Kaleidoscope, Spiral | As before: C*n*, D*n*, and the spiral similarity orbit |
| **Wallpaper** | All 17 plane groups: p1 p2 · pm pg cm pmm pmg pgg cmm · p4 p4m p4g · p3 p3m1 p31m p6 p6m. Operations are stored in fractional lattice coordinates and conjugated into world space, with copies covering the artboard. Oblique groups have adjustable cell height and angle; rectangular groups have adjustable height |
| **Hyperbolic {p,q}** | Reflection group of the (π/p, π/q, π/2) triangle in a Poincaré disk of chosen radius, built by breadth-first search outward from the centre. Turning **Reflections** off keeps only the rotation subgroup, which gives chiral, Circle Limit III-style tilings |
| **Circle inversion** | Group generated by inversions in a ring of *k* circles, plus an optional hub circle, up to a chosen word length. **Rotate with ring** adds the ring's own rotations, Rʲ ∘ w, so the figure keeps *k*-fold symmetry |

## Recursion (per stroke)

- **Nest**: Mᴸ ∘ G, so the whole figure repeats inside itself.
- **Branch**: each child is (g·M)ᴸ ∘ G, so every copy spawns a smaller figure. This works on top of any mode, hyperbolic and inversion included.
- **Per-level controls**: fade, hue shift, and layering (interleaved, children on top, or children beneath).
- **Wallpaper, hyperbolic and inversion** build the recursive motif first, then let the group place it. That way every tile carries the recursion:
  - nest: g ∘ Mᴸ
  - branch: g ∘ (M ∘ k)ᴸ, where k ranges over the symmetries fixing the centre. For p6m that's the 12 operations at the lattice origin; for {p,q}, the 2p rotations and reflections about the disk centre.
  - For the finite point groups this is the same as before.
- **Copy limit**: 50,000 copies per stroke, or 4,000 without instancing. When a level would exceed it, the panel shows how many copies that level needs and what to change.

## Warp (per stroke, applied to every copy)

The warps are swirl, twirl, spherical, bubble, eyefish, sinusoidal, horseshoe, waves, cylinder, power zⁿ, ripple and pinch/bulge. Each is defined as `centre + scale·V((p − centre)/scale)` and blended with the unwarped position by **Amount**. Warps that are symmetric about the centre (swirl, bubble, spherical, eyefish and others) keep rotational symmetry. Guides and snapping are warped along with the drawing.

## Stroke, width, overlaps

- **Per-point width**: the Width tool (W) sets a multiplier on each control point. The multiplier is interpolated along the spline and combined with pen pressure and taper.
- **Overlap** controls stacking where a stroke's copies cross each other, or where it crosses itself:
  - **Later part on top** or **Earlier part on top**: depth comes from the stroke's own arc length, so every copy resolves its crossings the same way.
  - **Weave**: every crossing between the stroke and its copies is detected. A parity union-find makes each crossing one-over-one-under, then makes the over/under alternate along the strand wherever that doesn't contradict a crossing. The result is one height profile shared by all copies, and **Flip weave** inverts it.
- **Draw order** keeps the old behaviour. Glow, neon and airbrush always blend.

## Fills

Fills are drawn with even-odd stencilling.
- **Colour fills**: flat, linear, radial, and dome (lit).
- **Texture fills**: noise, hatch, cross-hatch, dots, cells (Worley) and marble. Patterns live in each copy's local frame, so they follow the symmetry, and **Scale**, **Weight** and **Angle** control them.
- **Lit (dome)**: any fill can take dome lighting.

Fill edges get their own anti-aliasing band, drawn just outside the stencil.

## Layers

Layers have visibility, lock, opacity and blend mode: normal, multiply, screen, overlay, darken, lighten, add or difference. Normal layers at 100% opacity draw straight into the accumulation buffer. Every other layer renders offscreen and is composited with W3C separable blending. New strokes go on the active layer, and locked or hidden layers can't be drawn on or selected.

## Selection and groups

- **Selecting (Edit, V)**: click to select a stroke, Shift-click to add one, or drag on empty space to box-select.
- **Editing**: panel changes apply to the whole selection.
- **Groups (Ctrl+G)**: grouped strokes share one symmetry, recursion and warp setting. Clicking any member selects the whole group. Ctrl+Shift+G ungroups.

## Blending touching ends

With **Spline → Blend touching ends** on (the default, saved with the document), separate open strokes on the same layer whose ends touch render as one continuous line, without being merged. "Touching" means the ends are within 40% of the stroke width.
- **Exact meeting point:** both ends meet exactly at the midpoint of the two endpoints.
- **Smooth curve:** on Catmull-Rom strokes, each end's spline uses the neighbour's next control point as its tangent, so the curve passes through the join smoothly.
- **Shared vertex:** both strokes build the joining vertex from the same three samples, the neighbour's sample across the join included. The tangent, miter and position therefore match exactly, and there's no cap at the join. Polyline and B-spline joins keep a round cap, shaded as the tube continuing, so they don't open a gap at a corner.
- **Continuous effects:** the gradient (Along stroke, Bands) and the ribbon twist run along the whole chain. Head-to-head and tail-to-tail joins are handled.
- **No taper** at a linked end.

**Loops.** A line that closes on itself has no free end, so a start-to-end gradient would put the end colour right next to the start colour. This applies to a **Closed** stroke, and to a ring of touching strokes. Loops therefore repeat seamlessly:
- **Along stroke** is mirrored: A, then B halfway round, then back to A.
- **Bands** fit a whole number of bands around the loop.
- **Ribbon twist** rounds to a whole number of turns.

Tested result: two touching Catmull-Rom strokes render pixel-identical to one merged stroke at the join. That holds for tail-to-head joins, head-to-head joins, and ends placed 6 px apart by hand.

## Joining strokes

Separate strokes that meet show a hard seam: each one runs its own gradient from start to end, and their round caps overlap. With **Spline → Join ends** on (the default), starting or ending a new line on an open stroke's end (shown by an amber ring) continues that stroke instead:
- **Starting on an end** extends that stroke. Extending from a stroke's start prepends the new points, so its direction and gradient don't flip.
- **Ending on another stroke's end** bridges the two into one stroke.
- **Ending on the same stroke's other end** closes the loop.
- **Taps followed by a drag** become one stroke.

**Join** (Ctrl+J) merges selected open strokes that you've already drawn, chaining them by nearest endpoints into the primary stroke and keeping its style. Joining works on each stroke's original (un-copied) instance, on the active layer.

## Snapping (S)

The pen, freehand start and end points, and handle drags all snap. Points take priority over lines. The snap targets are:
- the symmetry centre and the recursion anchors
- lattice nodes, cell centres and edge midpoints
- inversion circle centres, p-gon centres and vertices in the hyperbolic disk
- guide lines: mirrors, rays, lattice lines, circles and hyperbolic edges

The Centre tool snaps to artboard landmarks: centre, thirds, edges and corners.

## Files

Files are saved as JSON with format `recurror-doc`, version 2. Recurror v1 and Strophe files still open: layers and groups get sensible defaults, and the old **Tile** mode becomes a wallpaper group (p1, or pmm when cells were mirrored).
